# 10KP Content Policy — U-M Statement of Student Rights & Responsibilities (SSRR)

Source: University of Michigan Statement of Student Rights and
Responsibilities, effective October 16, 2025 (Ann Arbor). This file is a
policy-focused distillation for the moderation model — it captures the
behavioral rules a pitch might violate, not the resolution or appeals
process. Full document: contact Student Resolution Resources
(studentresolutionresources@umich.edu).

## Scope

The SSRR applies to any behavior that:

1. Occurs on University-owned or controlled property;
2. Occurs in the City of Ann Arbor;
3. Occurs at any University-sponsored or affiliated event or program —
   **including remote or online events**;
4. Occurs anywhere, **including remotely or online**, if it poses an
   immediate danger to another person.

A pitch submitted to the 10,000 Pitches gallery is a University-affiliated
online activity, so its content is fully within scope.

## Universal values

Submissions must uphold the University's stated values: civility,
dignity, diversity, education, equality, equity, freedom, honesty, and
safety. The University protects freedom of expression, including
unpopular views and dissent, but that right runs both ways — a pitch may
not deny that same right to others.

## Prohibited behaviors (SSRR §IV Violations)

Flag any pitch that itself constitutes, promotes, or provides
instructions for the following. These are quoted / paraphrased directly
from the SSRR:

- **A. Physical harm or threats.** Physically harming or directly
  threatening harm of any person, group, or animal in a manner that
  would cause a reasonable person to fear for physical safety.
- **B. Sexual misconduct.** As defined by the U-M Policy and Procedures
  on Student Sexual and Gender-based Misconduct and Other Forms of
  Interpersonal Violence.
- **C. Hazing.** As defined by the U-M Policy Against Hazing.
- **D. Stalking.** As defined by the sexual/gender-based misconduct policy.
- **E. Intimate partner violence** (dating or domestic violence).
- **F. Harassment, bullying, or doxxing.** Physically, verbally, or
  through any other means, including online. Includes discrimination,
  harassment, and retaliation as defined by the interim U-M policy.
- **G. Tampering with fire/safety equipment** or setting unauthorized
  fires.
- **H–K. Illegal alcohol / drugs.** Possessing, using, distributing,
  manufacturing, or selling either. Legal, regulated industries
  (breweries, licensed dispensaries, pharma) discussed as businesses are
  fine; step-by-step instructions for illegal supply are not.
- **L. False emergency reports** (bombs, fires, other emergencies).
- **M. Theft, vandalism, damage, defacement** of University property or
  the property of others.
- **N. Obstructing classes, research, activities, or facilities**
  (except behavior protected by U-M's Freedom of Speech and Artistic
  Expression policy).
- **O. Falsified or altered University documents/records**; knowingly
  providing false information to a University official.
- **P. Identity assumption / impersonation** without proper
  authorization. Explicitly includes AI-generated or synthetic content
  that impersonates another individual or organization without
  authorization.
- **Q. Refusing lawful requests to leave** University-controlled premises.
- **R. Off-campus criminal conviction / plea** where the underlying
  behavior impacts the University community.
- **S. Misusing or interfering with SSRR resolution procedures.**
- **T. Violation of other University rules** (Housing, IT, Recreation,
  Student Organization policies).
- **U. Firearms, explosives, or weapons** on University property or at
  University events (unless approved by Public Safety).
- **V. Bias-motivated misconduct.** Any of the above motivated by bias
  or prejudice against the classes listed in the U-M Nondiscrimination
  Policy: **race, color, national origin, age, marital status, sex,
  sexual orientation, gender identity, gender expression, disability,
  religion, height, weight, or veteran status**. Outcomes are enhanced
  when bias motivation is present — flag both the underlying behavior
  and the bias motivation.

## Additional safety categories (for the moderation model)

Beyond SSRR violations, hold for review any content that would clearly
not belong in a public University gallery:

- Self-harm content (promotion of suicide, self-injury, disordered eating).
- Third-party personal information (home addresses, government IDs,
  financial account details, private contact info).
- Spam or clearly irrelevant submissions (not the student's own work,
  or purely promotional content unrelated to the competition).

## Legitimate-context safe harbor

Do **not** flag a pitch merely because it mentions a sensitive topic in
a legitimate academic, entrepreneurial, medical, historical,
journalistic, or public-safety context. Examples:

- A public-health pitch may discuss addiction, suicide prevention, or
  firearm-injury statistics.
- A business pitch may reference a legal-but-regulated industry
  (cannabis, firearms, alcohol, gambling) without being flagged.
- A research pitch may cite violent historical events.

The rule of thumb: flag a pitch when the **pitch itself** violates a
rule, not when it merely discusses a sensitive subject.

## When in doubt

Prefer routing borderline submissions to human review rather than either
approving or rejecting them automatically. Human reviewers have context
(the student's other work, the competition's tone, prior submissions)
that automated moderation does not.
